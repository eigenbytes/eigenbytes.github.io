TL;DR

VFL lets organizations holding different features about the same users train a joint model without sharing raw data. Compared with horizontal FL (same features, different users), VFL exchanges intermediate computations to protect privacy, and typically runs in cross-silo settings (e.g., bank + retailer). The 2024 survey maps the space—protocols, efficiency tricks, learning with limited labels, attack/defense patterns, and a unifying design framework called VFLow. 1) What is VFL?

In Vertical Federated Learning, parties share the same users (IDs) but own different columns (features). Contrast:

  • HFL — same features, different users.
  • VFL — same users, different features (our focus).
  • FTL — both users and features differ with limited overlaps.

Source: survey definitions and taxonomy. [oai_citation:1‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

2) The VFL training protocol

  1. Private Entity Alignment (PSI) — find the intersection of user IDs without revealing non-overlaps.
  2. Collaborative Training — each party computes local outputs; the active party computes the loss, sends gradients back; parties update locally (SGD/GBDT, etc.).

These two phases (alignment → training) structure nearly all VFL systems. [oai_citation:2‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

3) Model variants: splitVFL vs aggVFL

  • splitVFL: the active party owns a trainable global module (split neural networks across parties).
  • aggVFL: the active party uses a non-trainable aggregator (e.g., sigmoid, optimal split in trees).
  • Tree-based VFL (GBDT/RF) is widely used; local “partial trees” aggregate into a full tree during training.

Variant taxonomy and tree-based specifics summarized in the survey. [oai_citation:3‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

4) Making VFL efficient

Multiple Local Updates

Reduce syncs by doing several local steps per round (e.g., FedBCD / time-bounded local steps). Trade-off: more compute per party vs fewer comms.

Asynchronous Coordination

Upload/download out of lock-step to shrink idle time; control staleness (drop unstable updates, bound delays, tree-structured comms).

One-Shot Training

Learn local latent representations once, then the active party trains on shared representations. Beware privacy of those representations.

Compression & Selection

Dimensionality reduction (PCA/autoencoders), sparse/quantized messages, gradient packing; sample/feature selection to shrink payloads.

Communication tricks and their trade-offs cataloged extensively. [oai_citation:4‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

5) Making VFL effective with limited labeled overlap

Real deployments often have few aligned + labeled samples. The survey shows four families that squeeze more signal from the data you already have.

  1. Self-Supervised — contrastive/autoencoding on unlabeled & unaligned data to pretrain local encoders.
  2. Semi-Supervised — pseudo-label & complete missing features to expand the training set safely.
  3. Knowledge Distillation — teach the active party a local predictor (useful when collaborative inference is impractical).
  4. Transfer Learning — treat active party as source domain; adapt knowledge to passive parties with few/no labels.

Coverage of Self-SL, Semi-SL, KD, and TL under VFL constraints. [oai_citation:5‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

6) Privacy threats & defenses

Security protocols (P-levels)

  • P-0 (relaxed): labels or models may be non-private (e.g., white-box models).
  • P-1 (basic): keep data & models local; exchange plaintext intermediates.
  • P-2: encrypt transmitted intermediates (HE/MPC/TEE hybrids).
  • P-3: protect the entire training protocol (no training info revealed; MPC/SPDZ, etc.).
  • P-4: also protect learned models (only final inference exposed).

Progression of privacy guarantees and representative schemes. [oai_citation:6‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

Attack surface

  • Label inference: gradients (sample-level or batch-level), trained local models, spectral/model-completion attacks.
  • Feature inference: model/gradient inversion (white-box/black-box), reverse multiplication/protocol-aware attacks; tree-specific leakage.

Comprehensive taxonomy of label/feature inference attacks and when they apply. [oai_citation:7‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

Defenses

Cryptographic

HE, MPC, SS, TEE, and hybrids; tailored protocols for LR/NN/GBDT; consider efficiency vs privacy envelope.

Non-crypto

Differential privacy noise, gradient discretization/sparsification, masking/fake gradients; attack-specific defenses (e.g., CAE/DCAE, MARVELL, DRAVL).

7) Backdoor attacks & defenses

Passive parties can be malicious: they can implant targeted triggers (map trigger → label τ) or run non-targeted poisoning (adversarial/noisy samples, missing features) to degrade performance. Robust feature-space recovery, disguising labels (CAE/DCAE), and regularization help; note cryptographic protection alone doesn’t neutralize backdoors (it preserves poisoned computations, too).

Backdoor scenarios and defenses summarized in the survey. [oai_citation:8‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

8) Data valuation, explainability, fairness

  • Valuation: feature/party Shapley, MI-based methods (powerful but compute-heavy); used to split gains or pick contributors.
  • Explainability: credibility checks, counterfactuals, interpretable scorecards, explainable feature grouping—while balancing privacy.
  • Fairness: adversarial de-biasing and constrained optimization to remove sensitive leakage under VFL’s privacy limits.

Ecosystem building blocks for sustainable federations. [oai_citation:9‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

9) VFLow: a design checklist for reality

VFLow is the paper’s unifying framework to co-optimize utility, privacy, efficiency, and fairness—plus a risk-evaluation module for attacks/defenses. Use it as a backbone for choosing model partition, protocol (P-level), efficiency tricks, and policy controls.

Framework overview and meta-objective outlined in the survey. [oai_citation:10‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

10) Build-ready checklist

Before you start

  • Define use-case & labels (who is active? who needs local inference?)
  • Map overlap sizes (aligned-labeled vs aligned-unlabeled vs unaligned)
  • Pick model family (NN vs GBDT) and variant (split/agg)

Privacy & Policy

  • Select P-level that meets regulation (P-2+ recommended)
  • Threat-model label & feature inference; plan defenses
  • Define auditability (logs, attestations, consents)

Efficiency

  • Budget latency/bandwidth; enable async or multi-local steps
  • Compress intermediates; consider representation learning
  • Failover for missing features at inference

Effectiveness

  • Pretrain encoders with Self-SL on unaligned data
  • Grow training set with Semi-SL (curated pseudo-labels)
  • Use KD if the active party needs solo inference

Federation Health

  • Data valuation for incentives (feature/party Shapley)
  • Explainability package (scorecards/counterfactuals)
  • Fairness constraints + bias monitoring

FAQ

Q: Who should be the active party?
A: The party that owns the labels and can host the aggregation/global module; it also bears most confidentiality risk—plan governance accordingly. [oai_citation:11‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

Q: Can I avoid collaborative inference later?
A: Yes—distill the joint model into a local predictor for the active party using KD techniques enumerated in the survey. [oai_citation:12‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)

Q: Do crypto defenses slow everything down?
A: They add overhead; that’s why async/one-shot/compression and careful protocol selection (P-2 vs P-3 vs P-4) matter. [oai_citation:13‡Vertical_Federated_Learning_Concepts_Advances_and_Challenges.pdf](file-service://file-TGYuPJXcyH9dRjuvTXjbuc)
